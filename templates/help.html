Help & How-To Guides

This app is designed to help you keep track of everything you need to do, so you can stop using your brain as a storage device. The main idea is simple:

  1. Capture Everything: Use the Inbox (or the "Add Task" button) to get thoughts and to-dos out of your head and into the system. Don't worry about organizing them yet, just get them in.
  2. Organize Your Tasks: From the Inbox, turn your thoughts into clear actions. Give them a name, and if they are part of a bigger goal, add them to a Project.
  3. Work from the Board: The Board is your main workspace. It shows you what's ready to be worked on, what you're currently doing, and what's done. Just drag tasks from left to right as you make progress!

Getting Things Done (GTD) is a personal productivity method created by David Allen. Its core promise is a calm, clear head: once you trust that every commitment is captured somewhere reliable, you can stop mentally rehearsing your to-do list and focus on what's in front of you. This app is built around GTD's five-step workflow:

  1. Capture: Collect anything that has your attention, tasks, ideas, worries, into a trusted system instead of your head. In this app, that's the Inbox. See the Brain Dump guides below for a deep dive on this step.
  2. Clarify: Decide what each captured item actually means. Is it actionable? If so, what's the very next physical step? This happens as you process items out of your Inbox.
  3. Organize: Put each item where it belongs, as a Task inside a Project, on a household List, parked in the Icebox for later, or filed under Someday/Maybe.
  4. Reflect: Step back regularly and make sure your system still matches reality. This is the job of the Weekly Review.
  5. Engage: With everything captured and organized, use your judgment to pick the best task to work on right now. This is what the Board is for.

You don't need to memorize this list, the app's structure walks you through it naturally. But recognizing these five steps will make it much easier to understand why each part of the app exists.

A Brain Dump is the practice of emptying every open loop in your head onto paper (or into your Inbox) in one sitting. In GTD terms, this is the first step of Capture taken to its fullest: rather than jotting down tasks one at a time as they occur to you, you set aside a few minutes to deliberately mine your mind for everything that's nagging at you.

This includes things like:

  • Unfinished tasks and errands ("renew the car registration").
  • Half-formed ideas or projects you've been meaning to start.
  • Nagging worries or "I should really..." thoughts.
  • Commitments you've made to other people.

The goal isn't to organize or judge these items as you write them, just to get them out of your head. Once they're captured in your Inbox, you can process each one, turning it into a Task, a Project, or a candidate for your Someday/Maybe list.

Doing a brain dump periodically (not just once) is what gives GTD its signature feeling of a "mind like water": clear and ready to focus, because you trust that nothing important is being forgotten.

Set aside 15-20 minutes of uninterrupted time, then work through it like this:

  1. Grab a fast capture tool. Open the Inbox (or use pen and paper if you'd rather write first and enter items later).
  2. Write one item at a time. As soon as something comes to mind, capture it in as few words as possible and move on. Don't stop to organize, prioritize, or judge it.
  3. Keep going until you run dry. Most people hit a wall after a few minutes, push past it. The best items often surface after the "obvious" ones are out.
  4. Use trigger prompts to jog your memory. If you get stuck, scan categories like:
  • Work projects, deadlines, and emails you're behind on.
  • Home and family: repairs, errands, appointments.
  • Financial or administrative to-dos (bills, renewals, filing).
  • People you owe a call, reply, or favor.
  • Things you're worried about or that feel "unfinished."
  • Goals or ideas you've been putting off.
  1. Stop and process. Once you're truly out of items, go through your Inbox one by one and turn each into a Task, add it to a Project, or send it to your Someday/Maybe list.

You don't need to get it perfect the first time. Brain dumps are most powerful as a repeated habit, do a quick one anytime you feel overwhelmed, and a fuller one as part of your Weekly Review.

It's a simple but important difference:

  • A Task is a single, physical action. "Call the plumber," "Buy milk," or "Draft the email."
  • A Project is any goal that requires more than one task to complete. "Renovate the Kitchen" is a project. The tasks would be "Hire a contractor," "Choose new cabinets," "Buy appliances," etc.

If you have a big goal, create a Project for it. Then, add all the small steps as tasks within that project. This keeps your board clean and helps you focus on one step at a time.

In GTD, a Context is a tag that describes where, when, or with what tool a task can actually get done, things like @computer, @errands, or @phone. The idea is that your next action isn't just "what should I do," it's "what can I do right now, given where I am and what's available to me?"

You can set a Context on any task. When you're at the hardware store, you don't want to scroll past forty unrelated tasks to find the three you can actually do there, you want a filtered list of exactly those three.

That's what the Context Report gives you: all of your active tasks grouped by context, so you can pick useful work based on your current situation instead of re-reading your entire task list every time.

The Lists section is perfect for simple checklists that aren't part of a big project. It's great for things like:

  • Grocery shopping lists
  • Packing lists for a trip
  • A list of movies to watch

You can create as many lists as you want. The "General Shopping List" is a special list that acts as a catch-all for quick items you need to buy.

The Icebox is a holding area for tasks that you've processed from your inbox, but aren't ready to actively work on yet.

Think of it as your "on deck" list. The tasks are defined and ready to go, but keeping them in the Icebox prevents your main Board from getting cluttered. When you're ready to start on a new batch of work, you can move items from the Icebox to the 'Ready' column.

This keeps your active board clean and focused only on what you've committed to doing soon.

Both statuses take a task off your active Board, but for different reasons, and GTD treats them differently because who's responsible for the next move is different:

  • Blocked means the task can't move forward until something else in your own system finishes first, usually another task. Mark a task Blocked and link it to the task it depends on; it'll reappear once that dependency is cleared.
  • Waiting For is GTD's term for anything you're expecting from someone else: a reply to an email, a part you ordered, an answer from a coworker. You've done your part; the ball is in someone else's court.

Keeping a real Waiting For list matters because these are exactly the commitments people quietly forget, they feel "done" to you since you took your action, but nothing will actually happen unless you follow up. Your Weekly Review surfaces both your Blocked and Waiting For tasks so you can check on them and nudge things along when needed.

The Someday/Maybe list is for things you might want to do, but not right now. It's a "low-pressure" list for incubating ideas.

Good candidates for this list are:

  • Trips you want to take someday (e.g., "Learn to scuba dive").
  • Books to read or movies to watch.
  • Projects you're not committed to yet (e.g., "Build a deck").

Putting these items here keeps them out of your active system, but ensures you won't forget about them. You can review this list periodically and activate items when the time is right.

The Weekly Review is arguably the most critical habit for making GTD work. It's the time you set aside each week to keep your system clean, current, and trusted.

The Review Page helps you with the three main steps:

  1. Get Clear: Process everything in your Inbox to zero.
  2. Get Current: Review your active projects and "next actions" to make sure they are still relevant. Check on items you're waiting for.
  3. Get Creative: Look over your Someday/Maybe list for any ideas you want to activate.

Doing this weekly ensures nothing falls through the cracks and you can trust that your system is a complete picture of your world.
